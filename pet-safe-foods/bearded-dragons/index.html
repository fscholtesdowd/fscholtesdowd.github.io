What can bearded dragons eat?

Bearded Dragons can eat: Watercress, rocket, chicory & cress; Grated butternut squash; Dandelion, clover & plantain leaves; Collard greens & okra; Crickets, locusts & calciworms; Fresh water in a large, shallow dish. Feed only a little: Cabbage & kale; Vitamin & mineral powders on live food; Clean children's play sand (healthy adults only). Never feed: Spinach; Calci-sand & loose chunky substrates (bark, wood chips, crushed walnut, corn cob); Live food bigger than the dragon's mouth; Uneaten live food left in the vivarium.

Every line below is traced to a named source (2 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for bearded dragons, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a bearded dragon looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • Young dragons need more livefood than adults, so give them about 65 percent livefood, 35 percent greens, increasing the amount of greens as they grow. Older bearded dragons (larger than 30cm) need about 40 percent livefood and 60 percent greens. (RSPCA)
  • Young dragons need more livefood than adults, so give them about 65 percent livefood, 35 percent greens, increasing the amount of greens as they grow. Older bearded dragons (larger than 30cm) need about 40 percent livefood and 60 percent greens. (RSPCA)
  • Feeder insects should also be 'gut-loaded' with vitamins and minerals by offering them an appropriate formulated gut-loading diet 24-48 hours prior to feeding them to your beardie. (RSPCA)
  • Baby dragons are fed twice daily with the greens chopped up small. Juveniles and adults are fed once daily, in the morning. (RSPCA)
  • Dust live food lightly with vitamin and mineral powder, follow the maker's directions, and with enough UV light you won't need high dietary D3. (RSPCA)
