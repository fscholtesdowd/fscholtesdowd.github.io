What can hamsters eat?

Hamsters can eat: A complete hamster pellet; Timothy hay; Leafy & garden greens to rotate - broccoli, cabbage, kale, spinach, celery; Root & squash veg to rotate - beetroot, parsnip, pumpkin, squash, cucumber, courgette; More veg in small amounts - bell peppers, cauliflower, brussels sprouts, peas, sweetcorn, mushroom; Safe fruit, tiny amounts - apple, banana, melon, pear, peach, tomato; Herbs to rotate in - parsley, coriander, sage. Feed only a little: Occasional treats - nuts, boiled egg, mealworms; Muesli-style seed mixes; Citrus fruit - skip for now; New foods & wet or powdered food; Less common veg - artichoke, asparagus, chicory, cress, ginger. Never feed: Grapes; Rhubarb; Chocolate & anything with caffeine; Avocado.

Every line below is traced to a named source (3 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for hamsters, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a hamster looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • A commercial rat or mouse pellet containing 15-20% protein meets their needs. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Treats should make up no more than 10% of the diet. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • A small daily portion of fruit, veg and herbs is great for adding variety to your hamster's diet - just a teaspoon or two a day will do. (PDSA)
  • Too much sugary fruit or veg can lead to obesity or diabetes in any hamster. Russian Dwarf hamsters are especially prone. (PDSA)
  • Avoid sudden dietary changes and stale food - both can upset a hamster's stomach. (RSPCA (UK))
