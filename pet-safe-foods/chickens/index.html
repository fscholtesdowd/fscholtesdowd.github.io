What can chickens eat?

Chickens can eat: Leafy greens & lettuce; Veggie peels & trimmings; Stale bread; Scratch grains - cracked corn, oats, wheat, barley; Grass & tender young plants; Bugs & grubs - crickets, grasshoppers, mealworms, fly larvae, earthworms; Free-range finds - seeds, greens & bugs they forage; Oyster shell - free choice, for laying hens; Grit - small stones for grinding food. Feed only a little: ALL treats together - the 20-minute rule; Scratch grains - about 10% of the day's food; Big piles of greens or lettuce; Fatty or sugary leftovers; Old, tough, fibrous plants. Never feed: Avocado - skin, pit & leaves; Chocolate, candy & sugar; Beans - raw, dry, or half-cooked; Green potato peels & sprouts; Rhubarb & nightshade leaves (tomato, potato, pepper, eggplant plants); Onions & garlic; Salty foods & salty scraps; Moldy or rotten anything; Raw yeast dough; Peach & cherry family pits and leaves; Alcohol & tobacco.

Every line below is traced to a named source (7 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for chickens, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a chicken looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • A complete feed from the feed store is the meal. Treats are extras on top. (Univ. of Kentucky Ext., Utah State Ext.)
  • Never give layer feed to chicks - the extra calcium damages their kidneys. (Univ. of Maryland Ext., UF/IFAS Extension)
  • Fresh clean water always - chickens drink about twice as much water as they eat feed. (Univ. of Kentucky Ext., Oregon State Ext.)
  • Oyster shell is calcium, not grit. It is too soft to grind food. (Univ. of Kentucky Ext., Oregon State Ext.)
  • Not sure about a food? Skip it, or ask your county Extension office - the advice is free. (UF/IFAS Extension)
