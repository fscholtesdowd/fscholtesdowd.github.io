What can ferrets eat?

Ferrets can eat: Ferret food (dry kibble); Pieces of meat; Boiled egg & cooked chicken; Freeze-dried muscle or organ meat; A food that fits the ferret's age and health; Fresh, clean water. Feed only a little: Treats of any kind; Cat or kitten food; Raw meat & raw-meat diets; Soft or wet food. Never feed: Chocolate; Sugary foods: honey, raisins & fruit; Grapes, raisins, sultanas & currants; Milk, dairy, bread & cereals; Plant-protein diets (including pea protein); Xylitol (sugar-free gum, mints, some peanut butter).

Every line below is traced to a named source (3 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for ferrets, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a ferret looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • Ferrets are carnivorous and require high levels of protein (35%-40%) in the diet. Fat content may vary, but both the carbohydrate (< 25%) and fiber (< 2.5%) proportions of the diet should be relatively low. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Ferrets are carnivorous and require high levels of protein (35%-40%) in the diet. Fat content may vary, but both the carbohydrate (< 25%) and fiber (< 2.5%) proportions of the diet should be relatively low. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Keep treats to a minimum, no more than 5% of a ferret's daily caloric intake. Too many treats may lead to malnutrition. Good choices include meat, eggs, and freeze-dried muscle or organ meat, sold as cat or dog treats. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Make sure your ferrets have constant access to clean drinking water, as they'll become seriously ill without it. Check their water supply twice a day (RSPCA)
  • Ferrets have a high metabolism and a short digestive tract, so they need to eat little and often, with food available at all times. (RSPCA, Merck Vet. Manual)
