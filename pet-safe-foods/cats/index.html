What can cats eat?

Cats can eat: A complete & balanced cat food (AAFCO statement); Canned (wet) cat food; Plain cooked chicken or turkey; Pumpkin; Carrots, peas, cucumber & asparagus; Blueberries, strawberries, banana & melon; Apple slices - no seeds; Fresh water. Feed only a little: Milk & dairy; Homemade or vegetarian meals; Any treat or snack, even one from this page's safe list. Never feed: Onions, garlic, chives & leeks; Grapes & raisins; Chocolate, coffee & caffeine; Alcohol & raw bread dough; Raw meat, raw eggs & raw fish; Canned fish sold for people (tuna, salmon, etc.); Raw bones; Dog food, used as a cat's regular food; Salt & salty snacks.

Every line below is traced to a named source (5 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for cats, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a cat looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • Cats are obligate carnivores - they need real animal protein, every day, for life. (Cornell Feline Health Center, PDSA)
  • Taurine is an essential amino acid for cats - they must get it from food, and it comes from meat. (Merck Vet. Manual, PDSA)
  • A good rule of thumb is not to let treats exceed 10 to 15 percent of a cat's daily caloric intake. (Cornell Feline Health Center)
  • Cats naturally eat several small meals a day, so split the day's food up. (RSPCA, PDSA)
  • Change foods slowly, over a couple of weeks. (PDSA)
