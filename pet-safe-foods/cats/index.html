What can cats eat?

Cats can eat: A complete & balanced cat food (AAFCO statement); Canned (wet) cat food; Plain cooked chicken or turkey; Plain canned pumpkin; Cooked, mashed sweet potato; Natural peanut butter; Apple slices - no seeds or core; Fresh water. Feed only a little: Milk & dairy; Homemade or vegetarian meals; Any treat or snack, even one from this page's safe list. Never feed: Onions, garlic, chives & leeks; Grapes & raisins; Chocolate, coffee & caffeine; Alcohol & raw bread dough; Raw meat, raw eggs & raw fish; Canned fish sold for people (tuna, salmon, etc.); Bones - raw or cooked; Dog food, used as a cat's regular food; Salt & salty snacks.

Every line below is traced to a named source (4 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for cats, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a cat looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • Cats are obligate carnivores - they need real animal protein, every day, for life. (Cornell Feline Health Center, VCA Animal Hospitals)
  • Taurine is an essential amino acid for cats - they cannot make enough of it themselves. (VCA Animal Hospitals)
  • The general rule is that 90% of your cat's daily calorie intake should come from their complete and balanced food, and the remaining 10% of calories can come from treats and snacks. (VCA Animal Hospitals)
  • Cats naturally graze - some eat 12 to 20 small meals a day if food is always out. (VCA Animal Hospitals)
  • Try a new food slowly, a small piece at a time. (VCA Animal Hospitals)
