What can guinea pigs eat?

Guinea Pigs can eat: Grass hay - timothy, orchard & other low-calcium hay; Bell peppers - red or green; Leaf lettuces - romaine, red leaf, green leaf; Tomato - the ripe fruit only; Cucumber; Greens to rotate - kale, collards, beet greens, cilantro, parsley, endive; Carrot tops - the leafy greens; Turnip, asparagus & squash; Fortified guinea pig pellets - a measured scoop. Feed only a little: Fruit - apple, pear, orange, kiwi, berries; Carrots - the orange root; Alfalfa hay & alfalfa pellets; Pellets past the scoop; Any NEW food. Never feed: Avocado - fruit, pit, stems & leaves; Chocolate, cocoa & anything with caffeine; Onion family - onion, garlic, chives, leeks; Iceberg & head lettuce; Seeds, nuts, oats & dry cereal; Candy, sugary treats & yogurt drops; Green potato parts & nightshade leaves; Meat, dairy & anything from an animal.

Every line below is traced to a named source (3 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for guinea pigs, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a guinea pig looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • In general, guinea pigs should ingest their body size in hay, approximately 1-2 teaspoons of pellets, and 1-2 cups of produce items per day, with a focus on those containing high amounts of vitamin C. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Guinea pigs need 10 mg to 50 mg of vitamin C per day. ... Guinea pigs cannot manufacture their own vitamin C, so they must get adequate vitamin C through their diet. ... A chronic lack of vitamin C can lead to scurvy (VCA Animal Hospitals)
  • Vitamin C drops in the water bottle are not recommended - they turn the taste bad, so pigs drink less. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Vitamin C in guinea pig pellets is relatively unstable and breaks down (oxidizes) quickly. Be sure to use or replace your guinea pig's pellets within 90 days of the date of manufacture. (VCA Animal Hospitals)
  • Signs of deficiency may include: A rough hair coat / Loss of appetite / Diarrhea / Reluctance to move or signs of pain / Swollen feet or joints / Hemorrhages or ulcers on the gums or skin ... Guinea pigs with vitamin C deficiency should be seen by a veterinarian as soon as possible. (VCA Animal Hospitals)
