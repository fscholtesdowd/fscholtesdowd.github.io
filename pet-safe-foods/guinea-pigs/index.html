What can guinea pigs eat?

Guinea Pigs can eat: Grass hay - timothy, orchard & other grass hay; Bell peppers - red or green; Leaf lettuces - romaine, red leaf, green leaf; Tomato - the ripe fruit only; Cucumber; A mix of veggies & greens - 5 or 6 kinds a day; Carrot tops - the leafy greens; Turnip greens - a vitamin C green; Fortified guinea pig pellets - a measured scoop. Feed only a little: Fruit - apple, pear, banana, kiwi, melon; Carrots - the orange root; Alfalfa hay & alfalfa pellets; Pellets past the scoop; Any NEW food. Never feed: Avocado - fruit, pit, stems & leaves; Chocolate, cocoa & anything with caffeine; Onion family - onion, garlic, chives, leeks; Iceberg lettuce; Lawnmower clippings; Candy, sugary treats & yogurt drops; Green potato parts & nightshade leaves; Meat, dairy & anything from an animal.

Every line below is traced to a named source (4 veterinary or animal-welfare sources, listed at the bottom). If a food is not on this page, no source we use covers it for guinea pigs, so we do not list it either way. This is a feeding guide, not vet care: if a guinea pig looks sick or stops eating, call a vet.

What the vets say

  • In general, guinea pigs should ingest their body size in hay ... and 1-2 cups of produce items per day, with a focus on those containing high amounts of vitamin C (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Guinea pigs, like humans and some other mammals, can't make vitamin C and must get it from their diet. Guinea pigs need at least 10 milligrams (mg) of vitamin C per kilogram (kg) of body weight per day (pregnant females need 30 mg per kg per day). Because most guinea pigs weigh around 1 kg, and vitamin C is safe even at higher doses, it's safe to give 10 mg/guinea pig and 30 mg to any pregnant female without worrying. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Vitamin C drops in the water bottle are not recommended - they turn the taste bad, so pigs drink less. (Merck Vet. Manual, PDSA)
  • Without enough vitamin C, guinea pigs can lose their appetite, develop dental problems, have swollen joints, bleed under the skin or in organs, become weak, and possibly die. Signs include rough coat, lameness, weight loss, diarrhea, and sudden illness. (Merck Vet. Manual)
  • Guinea pig teeth never stop growing. Chewing hay all day is what wears them down. (RSPCA, Merck Vet. Manual)
